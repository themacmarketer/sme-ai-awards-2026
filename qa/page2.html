02SCQA case study

351 words
SSituation

Lithan Academy provides adult education intended to bridge AI skills for the workforce. Its model is Competency Learning as a Service, which combines structured curricula with work-integrated learning. Course development is the production line: a module must map to competencies, carry content, assessments and rubrics, and survive quality review.

CComplication

A 60-hour module required around 300 staff hours to map competencies and prepare content, assessments and rubrics. Learners progressed at different speeds, and limited trainer capacity made timely, customised support difficult. Attendance, assignments and programme progress were tracked separately, so learner problems surfaced late. The organisation was constrained at both ends: slow to produce a course, and slow to notice a learner falling behind. Both constraints hit the same budget line, so investment in course production competed directly with learner support.

QQuestion

How could the academy produce courses faster and support each learner individually, without diluting curriculum quality or losing trainer oversight?

AAnswer

Lithan built Adaptive CLaaS, an AI-powered personalised learning system in three modules. CLaaS Developer uses generative AI to draft learning pathways, session plans, content and assessments, with AI-assisted quality checks and curriculum teams reviewing and refining the output. CLaaS Mentor adjusts pace and difficulty based on learner progress and recommends resources for identified gaps, while providing on-demand guidance, reminders and automated assessment feedback, and escalating learners who need more support. CLaaS Manager consolidates enrolment, attendance, assignment completion and programme progress into a single view. The citation reports 75% faster curriculum development, 60% less manual monitoring and support, and about 60% year-on-year revenue growth. The human-in-the-loop boundary is explicit: AI handles repetitive drafting and routine guidance, while curriculum teams and trainers remain responsible for content quality and complex interventions. The citation does not report learning outcomes or completion rates, so the effect on educational quality, as distinct from production speed, is unmeasured. Revenue growth is the least attributable figure in the citation, because course production speed is one input among several, alongside demand for AI skills, pricing, and the sales capacity to fill the additional courses produced. Completion and pass rates would show whether faster production improved learning or merely accelerated it.

Implementation takeaway

Generate the draft, not the decision. Curriculum teams kept authority over quality, which is what made the speed gain safe to sell.

07How another SME could build something similar

Advisory, not the winner's method

This section is guidance for other companies. It is not a description of what Lithan Academy did, and it is not endorsed by them.

Suitable for

  • · Training providers
  • · Corporate learning teams
  • · Universities and polytechnics
  • · Professional certification bodies

Minimum requirements

  • · A written competency framework
  • · Past courses with expert-reviewed versions as reference
  • · Trainers with authority to reject generated content

Three implementation paths

Level 1

Drafting assistant

Use a language model to draft session plans and quiz items from an existing outline.

Use when: Development time is the bottleneck.

Level 2

Structured generation pipeline

Generate against a competency framework with templates, rubrics and expert review.

Use when: Multiple modules and consistent quality are required.

Level 3

Adaptive learning system

Add pace adaptation, automated feedback and consolidated learner monitoring.

Use when: Learner support capacity is the constraint.

Suggested sequence

  1. 1Pick one module and measure current development hours
  2. 2Generate drafts against the competency framework with strict templates
  3. 3Have subject experts edit and record their changes
  4. 4Use the edits to tighten templates before scaling
  5. 5Add learner monitoring once content production is stable

Tools and alternatives

  • · Language model with long-context drafting
  • · Learning management system
  • · Assessment and rubric templates
  • · Analytics dashboard

Risks and PDPA considerations

  • · Generic content that passes review but teaches nothing
  • · Assessment items that leak answers
  • · Over-automation of feedback that learners need from a person

KPIs to track

  • Staff hours per module
  • Expert edit ratio
  • Learner completion rate
  • Assessment pass rate
  • Trainer hours per learner
